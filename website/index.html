GreenDC

Sustainable data centre management and optimisation across six dimensions: energy, water, cooling, carbon, noise and location. GreenDC turns operating data into PUE, WUE, CUE and acoustic indicators, combines them into one GreenScore, flags anomalies, and tests resource-saving scenarios.

Abstract

Data centres are essential to the digital economy, but their growing demand for electricity, water and cooling contributes to environmental challenges. Existing monitoring approaches often focus on individual efficiency metrics, which makes the overall environmental impact of a facility hard to understand.

GreenDC proposes an integrated, data-driven framework for assessing and improving data centre sustainability across six dimensions: energy consumption, water usage, cooling efficiency, carbon emissions, noise pollution and location-based environmental impact. The prototype processes publicly available and simulated datasets to calculate indicators such as Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE), Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) and estimated carbon emissions. A unified GreenScore summarises performance across the six dimensions, supported by visualisations, anomaly detection, recommendations and a what-if simulator.

The framework is designed to scale to real-time sensor data, multiple data centres and regional environmental datasets.

Decision support

Sustainability recommendations

Rules check each indicator against good practice. Every recommendation is then run through the simulator, so its savings and GreenScore gain come from your data. They are ranked by GreenScore gain.

GreenScore rewards efficiency ratios such as PUE, so a measure that saves a lot of energy without changing PUE (server consolidation, for example) ranks lower by score than by energy. Cost savings include carbon valued at the facility carbon price.

    Sensitivity analysis

    The GreenScore depends on two design choices: the dimension weights and the benchmark anchors. This page measures how much the results move when either choice changes.

    One weight at a time

    Each weight set to 0 and to double its default, others unchanged

    Benchmark anchors

    Every best and worst anchor is moved at random by up to the stated share of its span, and the GreenScore of the loaded facility is recalculated.

    Monte Carlo over weights: do location rankings hold?

    The same 2 MW evaporative facility is simulated in all eight regions. For each of 2,000 random weightings, GreenDC recomputes every GreenScore and ranks the regions, then compares that ranking with the default one.

    Scoring

    Each sub-indicator is mapped linearly to a 0–100 score between a best anchor (score 100) and a worst anchor (score 0), and clamped. A dimension score is the weighted mean of its sub-indicators; sub-indicators that cannot be computed from the data are left out and the remaining weights are renormalised.

    Simulation model

    • Outdoor temperature: T(d) = T̄ + A·cos(2π(d − d_peak)/365) + ε, ε ~ N(0, 2.2 °C).
    • IT power: P = P_idle + (P_max − P_idle)·u with P_idle = 0.5·P_max; utilisation u has weekday, growth and noise terms.
    • Free-cooling fraction: f = adoption ÷ (1 + e^((T − T_fc)/1.8)).
    • Chiller COP = max(1.8, 6 − 0.13(T − 10)) × system factor.
    • Cooling energy = E_IT·[(1 − f)/COP + 0.04f + 0.07]; the last term is CRAH and pump fans.
    • Distribution and lighting losses ≈ 8.5 % of IT energy plus a fixed 0.5 % of capacity.
    • Water = E_IT·(a + b·max(0, T − 5))·(1 − 0.6f), with a and b set by the cooling system.
    • Plant sound power: Lw = Lw_unit + 10·log₁₀(N) + 50·log₁₀(fan speed).
    • Injected faults: cooling fault ×1.55 cooling, water leak ×2.1 water, noise spike +9 dB, IT surge ×1.18, UPS fault ×1.9 losses.

    Simulator levers

    • Setpoint raise: cooling × (1 − 0.04·Δ), water × (1 − 0.02·Δ); economiser threshold + Δ.
    • Economiser boost: on days below the threshold, removes a share of cooling above a 0.07·E_IT floor.
    • Virtualisation: E_IT × (1 − r); cooling, losses and water scale with heat load; utilisation rises to u ÷ (1 − r), capped at 100 %.
    • Loss reduction: E_other × (1 − r) for UPS eco-mode and LED lighting.
    • Renewables: replaces RE% in the market-based emission factor.
    • Water reuse: potable water × (1 − r), with a cooling energy penalty of 7.5 % × r.
    • Noise mitigation: subtracts the attenuation in dB(A) from every daily level.
    • Carbon is valued at the facility carbon price (default USD 85 per tonne CO₂e, editable on the Data page).

    Limitations & references

    • Grid carbon intensity and grid renewable share are cited national 2024 values (OWID/Ember); US presets share the US national value. Water stress, climate, tariffs and contracted renewables are indicative and should be replaced with site data for a specific study.
    • Market-based emissions apply the grid average to the non-renewable share. The GHG Protocol asks for a residual-mix factor, which is usually higher, so this underestimates market-based emissions where residual mixes are published.
    • Free-cooling potential uses daily mean dry-bulb temperature. Economiser design uses hourly dry- and wet-bulb data, so hourly data gives more precise results.
    • Noise uses daily representative levels. Planning limits are often night-time LAeq values, so night-time data should be used where it exists.
    • The simulated dataset is synthetic. It shows how the framework behaves; it is not evidence about any real facility.
    • Noise uses free-field propagation only (no barriers, ground or air absorption as in ISO 9613-2).
    • Linear normalisation anchors are a design choice; adjust them in engine.js for other benchmarks.
